## SECTION 4: LiDAR vs Photogrammetry — The Cost Difference

### What is LiDAR?
LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) uses laser pulses fired from the drone. The lasers can penetrate through tree canopy and vegetation to map the actual ground surface underneath. It is the gold standard for accurate topographic surveys in forested terrain.

Equipment used: DJI Zenmuse L1 LiDAR (3 returns per pulse, 240,000 points per second)

**When to use LiDAR:**
- Forested or jungle land
- Any terrain with significant vegetation
- Construction sites needing precise cut-and-fill calculations
- Legal cadastral surveys requiring centimeter accuracy
- Any project where accurate ground elevation is critical

### What is Photogrammetry?
Photogrammetry uses overlapping aerial photographs to build a 3D model. It works well for open terrain but cannot see through tree cover.

**When to use Photogrammetry:**
- Open agricultural land
- Construction sites with no vegetation
- When visual orthomosaic maps are the main deliverable
- Lower budget projects on open terrain

### Cost Difference
LiDAR surveys are generally **30–50% more expensive** than photogrammetry because the sensor technology is more specialized. However, for most land in Costa Rica — which has significant tree cover — LiDAR is the only method that produces accurate results. Choosing photogrammetry to save money on forested land will result in inaccurate data that is unusable for engineering or legal purposes.

## SECTION 5: 6 Key Factors That Affect Drone Survey Cost

### Factor 1: Property Size
The larger the area, the more flight time and processing time required. Most companies charge per hectare, with a minimum base cost.

### Factor 2: LiDAR vs Photogrammetry
Covered above — LiDAR is more expensive but often required.

### Factor 3: Terrain Complexity
Steep mountainous terrain, irregular boundaries, and dense jungle require more careful mission planning, more ground control points, and longer processing time. Flat open land is the easiest and fastest to survey.

### Factor 4: Location in Costa Rica
All of Costa Rica can be surveyed. However, travel from San José to remote locations adds cost:
- Greater Metropolitan Area (GAM): minimal or no travel fee
- Guanacaste: travel fee applies
- South Pacific / Osa Peninsula: travel fee applies
- Caribbean coast: travel fee applies
- Very remote or roadless areas: may require accommodation

Travel costs are always clearly itemized in the quote — no surprises.

### Factor 5: Deliverable Requirements
Standard deliverables (topographic map, point cloud, DXF, GeoTIFF) are included. Special requests like:
- BIM (Building Information Modeling) files
- Volumetric calculations for earthworks
- CATASTRO-ready cadastral boundary files
- NDVI agricultural analysis maps
...may require additional processing time and cost.

### Factor 6: Urgency / Rush Processing
Standard turnaround: 3–7 business days after the flight.
Rush processing (48-hour turnaround): available at a premium for active real estate closings or time-sensitive construction projects.

## SECTION 6: Is a Drone Survey Worth the Cost?

In almost every case, yes. Here's why:

**vs Traditional Ground Survey:**
- A licensed topographer in Costa Rica charges more per hectare and takes longer
- They cannot access steep or dense terrain safely
- Data is delivered as physical maps, not digital files
- No 3D point cloud or digital terrain model

**For Real Estate:**
A $1,000 drone survey on a 5-hectare property can reveal terrain issues, drainage problems, encroachments, or boundary discrepancies that would cost tens of thousands of dollars to fix after purchase. It is cheap insurance.

**For Construction:**
Accurate pre-construction topographic data prevents costly earthwork surprises. A single unexpected terrain issue on a construction project typically costs far more than the survey itself.

**For Agriculture:**
Precise terrain models enable proper drainage planning, irrigation design, and crop health monitoring. These improvements deliver ROI that far exceeds the survey cost.

**The rule of thumb:** A drone survey typically pays for itself many times over by preventing expensive surprises downstream.
