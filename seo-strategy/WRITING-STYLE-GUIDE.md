# DroneSurveyCR.com — Writing Style Guide
*For all blog posts, landing pages, and geo-targeted articles*

---

## The Core Principle: Write Like a Local Expert Talking to a Friend

Every article should feel like it was written by someone who has actually flown a drone over Guanacaste cattle farms, mapped construction sites in Escazú, and dealt with the rainy season logistics of surveying land in the Osa Peninsula. Not a robot. Not a marketing department. A real person who knows Costa Rica and knows drones.

If a sentence sounds like it came from a brochure, rewrite it.

---

## Voice & Tone

**Sound like:** A knowledgeable friend who happens to be a drone survey expert. Warm, direct, confident — never salesy or stiff.

**Don't sound like:** A corporate website, an AI writing tool, a legal document, or a textbook.

### Practical rules:

- **Use "you" constantly.** The reader is always the subject. "You'll need a lidar survey if your land has heavy tree cover" beats "Lidar surveys are recommended for forested terrain."
- **Use contractions.** "You're," "it's," "we'll," "that's." They make the writing feel human.
- **Short sentences win.** Aim for an average sentence length of 15 words or fewer. Mix short punchy sentences with longer ones for rhythm.
- **One idea per paragraph.** Three to four sentences max. White space is your friend — it makes the page feel easy to read.
- **Ask questions.** "Not sure whether you need lidar or photogrammetry? Here's how to decide." Questions pull readers forward.
- **Be specific to Costa Rica.** Mention real places, real terrain, real situations. "Dense jungle cover like you find in the Osa Peninsula" is 10x better than "forested areas."
- **Admit limitations honestly.** If something costs more, say so and explain why. Readers trust honesty far more than polish.
- **End sections with a forward pull.** Each section should make the reader want to read the next one.

## What to Avoid (Red Flags)

❌ "Leveraging cutting-edge technology to deliver best-in-class solutions"
✅ "We use a DJI Zenmuse L1 lidar sensor — the same equipment used on major infrastructure projects across Central America."

❌ "Comprehensive topographic mapping services are available throughout the region"
✅ "We survey everywhere in Costa Rica — from San José to the Osa Peninsula."

❌ "It is important to note that terrain complexity may impact overall project costs"
✅ "Steep mountain terrain takes longer to fly and process, so it costs a bit more."

❌ Starting sentences with "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Additionally," "It is worth noting"
✅ Just say the next thing.

❌ Passive voice: "The data is processed by our team"
✅ Active voice: "Our team processes your data"

❌ Vague claims: "We provide high-quality results"
✅ Specific claims: "We deliver centimeter-accurate topographic maps within 5 business days"

## Keyword Strategy: Natural Density, Not Stuffing

**Target density:** Primary keyword appears once every 150–200 words. That's roughly 5–7 times in a 1,000-word article. Any more and Google's systems flag it as unnatural.

**How to use keywords naturally:**

- Put the primary keyword in: H1, first paragraph, one H2, meta title, meta description, and one image alt tag. That's it for exact match.
- Use **semantic variations** for the rest of the article. Google understands synonyms. Examples:
  - "drone survey Costa Rica" → also use: aerial survey, drone mapping, lidar survey, topographic mapping, drone mapping service
  - "Guanacaste drone survey" → also use: aerial mapping in Guanacaste, drone services Liberia, survey Nicoya Peninsula
- **Never repeat the exact same phrase back-to-back.** If you used "drone survey in San José" in the previous paragraph, use "aerial mapping in the capital" next.
- Keywords should appear where they make sense grammatically. If you're forcing a keyword into a sentence and it sounds awkward, rewrite the sentence.

## Article Structure (All Blog Posts)

### Standard format:

```
H1: [Main keyword] — written as a question or clear promise
  ↓
Opening hook (2–3 sentences): A relatable situation or surprising fact. No keyword stuffing here — just pull the reader in.
  ↓
Quick Answer box: Bold, 2–3 sentences. Give the key takeaway upfront. Readers who skim will appreciate this, and Google often pulls it for featured snippets.
  ↓
H2 sections (4–6 sections): Each covers one clear sub-topic. Use natural keyword variations in H2s.
  ↓
FAQ section (3–5 questions): Real questions people ask. Use exact-match long-tail keywords here — this is your featured snippet bait.
  ↓
CTA (Call to Action): Simple, low-pressure. "Ready to get a quote? Fill out our 5-minute form and we'll get back to you within one business day."
```

### Word count targets:
- Standard service/topic post: **900–1,200 words**
- Geo-targeted town/province article: **700–900 words** (focused, not padded)
- Pillar/comparison post (e.g., Lidar vs Photogrammetry): **1,400–1,800 words**

## Geo-Targeted Article Rules

These articles target searches like "drone survey Guanacaste" or "lidar mapping Limón Costa Rica." They need to feel genuinely local, not like a template with the city name swapped in.

**Each geo article must include:**

1. **Something real and specific about that location** — terrain type, common land uses, typical projects (coffee farms, cattle ranches, beach real estate, construction). If it's Guanacaste, mention the dry season and how that affects survey timing. If it's the Caribbean coast, mention the rainfall and access challenges.

2. **A local use case.** "Cattle ranchers in Guanacaste use our drone surveys to measure pasture areas and plan new fence lines" is better than "we serve agricultural clients in the region."

3. **The province name AND at least 2–3 town/canton names.** Google rewards specificity. Mention Liberia, Nicoya, Tamarindo, Santa Cruz for Guanacaste. Don't just say "Guanacaste."

4. **Practical info relevant to that area.** Distance from San José affects travel fees — be honest about it. Rainy season timing differs by region. These details build trust.

5. **Internal links** to the most relevant service page (lidar mapping, photogrammetry, or geopositioning).

## SEO Checklist for Every Article

Before publishing, confirm:
- [ ] Primary keyword in H1 (exact match, once)
- [ ] Primary keyword in first 100 words of body text
- [ ] Primary keyword in meta title (under 60 characters)
- [ ] Primary keyword in meta description (under 155 characters)
- [ ] Keyword variations used naturally throughout
- [ ] At least one internal link to a service page
- [ ] At least one internal link to another blog post
- [ ] Image alt text contains keyword
- [ ] FAQ section with 3+ questions targeting long-tail keywords
- [ ] Schema markup: BlogPosting + FAQPage JSON-LD
- [ ] Canonical URL set correctly
- [ ] Reading level: aim for Grade 8 (Hemingway App score)
- [ ] No sentence starts with "Furthermore," "Moreover," "In conclusion"
- [ ] CTA with link to /quote.html
