The Costa Ballena Is Booming — and Building Here Is Harder Than It Looks

The stretch of South Pacific coast known as the Costa Ballena — anchored by Dominical, Uvita, and Ojochal — has become one of Costa Rica's most sought-after eco-tourism destinations. Marino Ballena National Park, the famous "Whale's Tail" sandbar, year-round whale watching, and a relaxed jungle-meets-ocean lifestyle have drawn a steady wave of boutique hotels, wellness retreats, vacation-rental villas, and residential communities. Land values have climbed accordingly, and so has the pace of development.

But the terrain that makes this coast beautiful is also what makes it difficult to develop. Properties climb steep, forested ridgelines that deliver the ocean views buyers pay for — while hiding creeks, ravines, unstable slopes, and elevation changes that no boundary map shows. Traditional ground surveys are slow, expensive, and sometimes outright dangerous on this terrain, and they often miss the very features that decide whether a lot is buildable. For developers, architects, and investors on the Costa Ballena, a drone survey is the fastest way to understand a property before money goes into the ground.

Why it matters here: On a steep Uvita hillside, a few metres of misjudged elevation can mean a villa positioned in a stream setback, an access road that exceeds legal grade, or a septic field that fails SETENA review. A precise terrain model up front prevents costly redesigns — and permit rejections — later.

LiDAR, not just photos — because of the canopy

Much of the Costa Ballena is covered in dense secondary and primary forest. A photogrammetry camera only records what it can see, and on a forested lot that is a roof of leaves — not the ground where you'll actually build. Our LiDAR sensors emit up to three returns per laser pulse: the first maps the canopy top, intermediate returns catch mid-story growth, and the last return reaches bare earth. The result is a true bare-ground terrain model even under closed canopy — the single most important deliverable for siting buildings and roads on this coast.

Case Study: A Hillside Eco-Villa Project Above Uvita

A developer assembling a boutique eco-villa project on a 6-hectare parcel in the hills above Uvita came to us with a common Costa Ballena problem: a stunning property, a beautiful concept render, and almost no reliable data about the land itself. The catastro (cadastral) plan showed boundaries and a declared area, but nothing about the steep, forested interior where the villas were meant to go.

We flew the full parcel with a LiDAR drone in a single morning, mobilising an RTK GPS base station to lock the data to real-world coordinates at ±2cm. Within 72 hours the team had a complete picture: a bare-earth digital elevation model under the canopy, 0.5-metre contours, a slope map, and a hydrological flow map showing two seasonal creeks the owner hadn't known crossed the property.

The outcome: Those two creeks carried legal setback requirements that would have placed three of the planned villas in protected riparian buffers. Because the issue surfaced in the survey rather than during SETENA review, the architect simply shifted the building pads uphill and re-routed the access road to a gentler grade — turning a potential permit rejection and redesign into a half-day adjustment, and producing topographic data that went straight into the EIA submission.

This is the recurring value of surveying first on the Costa Ballena: the data either confirms your concept or saves you from building it wrong. For buyers still in due diligence, the same flight verifies declared area, reveals true buildable zones, and prices the earthwork before an offer is finalised.

Costa Rica DGAC Drone Regulations — What Developers Should Know

All commercial drone operations in Costa Rica are regulated by the Dirección General de Aviación Civil (DGAC). Engaging an operator who flies legally protects your project: data collected by an unlicensed operator can be challenged, and incidents create liability for the landowner. Our operations comply with current DGAC requirements, which apply consistently across every region we serve:

Registered aircraft and certified remote pilots · operations kept within the standard altitude ceiling unless specific authorisation is held · respect for controlled and restricted airspace · and the appropriate SINAC / national-park authorisations before flying near protected areas such as Marino Ballena National Park. Full details are in our 2026 DGAC drone regulations guide.

On the Costa Ballena this matters in two specific ways: the airspace around Marino Ballena National Park and the 200-metre Zona Marítimo Terrestre (maritime zone) along the beachfront. We survey right up to and across the ZMT boundary for concession and setback work, and we coordinate SINAC authorisation before any flight near park airspace — so your data is gathered lawfully and stands up to scrutiny.

Frequently Asked Questions — Uvita & Ojochal Drone Surveys

Why do eco-tourism developers in Uvita and Ojochal need a drone survey before building?

The Costa Ballena's steep, jungle-covered terrain hides creeks, ravines, and slope changes that determine where you can legally and safely build. A LiDAR drone survey produces an accurate terrain model under the canopy so architects can position villas, access roads, and septic fields correctly, avoid setback violations near streams, and prepare the topographic data SETENA requires for an environmental viability permit.

Can you survey steep hillside lots above Uvita and Dominical?

Yes. Steep ocean-view hillsides are exactly where drone surveys outperform ground crews. We fly the full slope safely from the air, capturing dense elevation data on terrain that is dangerous or impractical to walk. Slope and aspect maps then show buildable zones, drainage paths, and where retaining or cut-and-fill work will be needed.

Do you provide the topographic data SETENA requires for an EIA on the Costa Ballena?

Yes. Our deliverables — digital elevation model, contour lines, hydrological flow maps, slope analysis, and accurate area calculations — meet standard SETENA Environmental Impact Assessment (Estudio de Impacto Ambiental / D1) submission requirements for projects in the Osa and Aguirre cantons.

How close to the maritime zone can you survey near Playa Hermosa and Playa Ventanas?

We survey right up to and across the 200-metre Zona Marítimo Terrestre (ZMT) boundary, producing the precise coastal topography and setback lines concession holders and municipalities need. We do not fly inside Marino Ballena National Park airspace without the appropriate SINAC authorisation.
