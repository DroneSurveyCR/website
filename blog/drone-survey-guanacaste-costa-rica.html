Cattle Ranch Mapping: The Most Common Guanacaste Project

Guanacaste is Costa Rica's ranching heartland. The province produces the majority of the country's beef, and cattle operations here range from modest 20-hectare family farms to commercial operations spanning 500+ hectares. For ranches of any size, a drone survey provides data that transforms how you manage the land.

Here's what ranch owners typically get from a lidar drone survey:

Topographic Maps with Contour Lines

Knowing the exact elevation profile of your pastures matters for drainage, road planning, and identifying areas prone to seasonal flooding. Our lidar data generates contour maps at intervals as fine as 25 centimeters — far more detail than you'd get from any publicly available topographic data.

Water Feature and Drainage Mapping

Seasonal streams, stock ponds, low-lying areas that flood during the rainy season — our elevation models map all of these accurately. This data is critical for planning new water infrastructure, designing cattle crossings, or siting buildings and corrals away from flood zones.

Fence Line and Boundary Verification

Ranch boundaries in Guanacaste don't always match what's recorded in the Registro Nacional. Fence lines drift over decades, neighbors encroach, and old survey markers disappear. A drone survey gives you a current, GPS-referenced picture of your property's actual boundaries and internal divisions.

Pasture and Land Use Classification

With aerial imagery and elevation data, you can clearly distinguish active pasture, forested areas, riparian zones, and degraded land. This information supports INDER land-use compliance, conservation set-aside planning, and decisions about which areas to clear, rest, or improve.

Example project: A 180-hectare cattle ranch near Hojancha needed a complete topographic survey for a farm modernization plan. The property had rolling terrain, scattered Guanacaste trees, and three seasonal streams. We completed the lidar flight in one day and delivered a full topographic map with 50cm contour lines, a drainage analysis, and a georeferenced orthomosaic — all within 5 business days. The data was used to plan new paddock divisions, a water catchment system, and an access road to the back pastures.

Real Estate and Land Development Surveys

Guanacaste's Pacific coast has seen steady real estate growth over the past decade, and it shows no signs of slowing. International buyers purchasing coastal land near Tamarindo, Nosara, Flamingo, and the Papagayo area face a common problem: the properties they're considering often have limited or outdated survey data.

A drone survey before purchase gives you the information you need to evaluate a property properly:

  • Precise boundary data overlaid on aerial imagery, so you can see exactly what you're buying
  • Slope analysis showing which areas are buildable and which exceed safe construction gradients
  • Elevation models that reveal drainage patterns, potential erosion areas, and flood risk zones
  • Setback compliance — coastal properties in Costa Rica must respect the Maritime Zone (Zona Marítimo Terrestre), and accurate mapping helps confirm your buildable area

For developers subdividing larger parcels, our surveys provide the topographic data your engineers need for site planning, road layout, and infrastructure design. We deliver files in all standard formats — DXF, SHP, GeoTIFF, LAS/LAZ — compatible with AutoCAD, ArcGIS, QGIS, and all major engineering software.

Agriculture: Beyond Cattle

While ranching dominates Guanacaste's landscape, the province also has significant melon, sugarcane, rice, and teak farming operations. Drone surveys support these operations in several ways:

  • Irrigation planning: Accurate elevation data is essential for designing gravity-fed irrigation systems, especially in the dry Pacific lowlands where water management is critical during the dry season (November through April)
  • Drainage design: Rice paddies and low-lying sugarcane fields need precise grading data to manage water flow during the rainy season
  • Teak and reforestation projects: Lidar penetrates tree canopy to map the ground surface beneath established plantations — something photogrammetry cannot do
  • SENARA compliance: Properties within irrigation districts managed by SENARA benefit from having current, accurate topographic data for water rights and infrastructure planning

If you're managing agricultural land in Guanacaste, our complete guide to drone surveys for agriculture in Costa Rica covers the full range of applications.

Guanacaste's Dry Season Advantage

One of the practical benefits of working in Guanacaste is the province's long, reliable dry season. From late November through April, Guanacaste typically sees almost no rain, making it ideal for drone survey operations. Clear skies mean consistent flying conditions, and the reduced vegetation during dry months improves lidar ground penetration in forested and scrubby areas.

We can and do fly during the rainy season as well — most days have clear mornings with rain arriving in the afternoon — but if you have flexibility in your timeline, scheduling your survey during the dry season gives the best results.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a drone survey cost in Guanacaste?

Pricing starts at $1,000 USD for sites up to 5 hectares, with each additional hectare at $80 USD. A travel fee from San José applies — typically $150–$300 depending on your exact location in Guanacaste. For large ranches over 100 hectares, we offer custom pricing. We include the exact travel fee in your quote so there are no surprises.

Can you survey large cattle ranches?

Yes. We regularly survey ranches of 50 to 500+ hectares across Guanacaste. Our DJI Zenmuse L1 lidar system covers large areas efficiently — a 100-hectare property typically requires one to two days of flight time. For ranches, we deliver topographic maps with contour lines, boundary data, water feature mapping, and terrain models useful for pasture planning and infrastructure siting.

What areas in Guanacaste do you cover?

All of Guanacaste province — from Liberia and La Cruz in the north down to Nicoya, Santa Cruz, Hojancha, and Nandayure in the south. We also serve all coastal towns including Tamarindo, Flamingo, Nosara, Sámara, Playas del Coco, and the Papagayo Peninsula.

Do I need a drone survey before buying land in Guanacaste?

A drone survey is strongly recommended before purchasing rural or coastal land in Guanacaste. Many properties have outdated or inaccurate boundary records, and the terrain can hide drainage problems, flood-prone areas, or steep slopes that aren't visible during a property visit. A lidar survey gives you precise elevation data, accurate boundaries, and a detailed terrain model — the information you need to make an informed purchase decision.

What about the Maritime Zone for coastal properties?

Coastal properties in Costa Rica are subject to the Zona Marítimo Terrestre (ZMT), which restricts development within 200 meters of the high-tide line. Our drone surveys provide the accurate mapping data needed to determine your property's relationship to the ZMT boundary. This is especially important in Guanacaste's beach towns where buildable area can be significantly affected by setback requirements.
