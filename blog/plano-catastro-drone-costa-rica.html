Plano Catastro with a Drone in Costa Rica: Everything You Need to Know

April 11, 2026 · Updated June 6, 2026 · 12 min read
Home / Blog / Plano Catastro with a Drone

In Costa Rica, drone technology has revolutionized the way cadastral survey maps — known locally as planos catastro — are created. Aerial surveys deliver accuracy, speed, and cost-effectiveness that traditional ground methods cannot match. If you need a plano catastro of your property for legal purposes, registration, or development, this guide explains everything you need to know about the drone-based process in Costa Rica.

What is a Plano Catastro?

A plano catastro is the technical and legal document that shows a property's exact boundaries, dimensions, total area, and geographic location. Costa Rica's Registro Nacional (National Registry) requires it for property registration, sales, land subdivision, and legal procedures.

Why Drones Are Better for Cadastral Surveys

Drone technology has transformed cadastral surveying in Costa Rica. Our DJI Matrice 300 RTK with the Zenmuse L1 sensor performs surveys with centimeter-level RTK accuracy, meeting the precision standards required by the Registro Nacional.

Centimeter-Level RTK Accuracy

Our aircraft's RTK (Real Time Kinematic) system delivers horizontal accuracy of 2–3 cm and vertical accuracy of 5 cm — better than the 10–15 cm typical of traditional theodolite or conventional GPS surveys. This accuracy is certifiable and legally accepted for cadastral registration.

Speed of Execution

A cadastral drone survey that takes 2–4 hours of flying and processing would have required 1–2 weeks with traditional ground methods. For properties of 5–50 hectares, drones dramatically reduce time in the field.

Full Coverage of Difficult Terrain

Many Costa Rican properties include mountainous terrain, dense vegetation, or limited access. Drones fly over these obstacles and capture complete data without needing ground access to every corner of the property. That lowers both cost and safety risk.

Visual Documentation

Beyond the technical map, drones produce high-resolution orthomosaics (2–5 cm per pixel) that document the property's actual condition. This is especially useful for detecting structures, access roads, and terrain features.

How a Drone Plano Catastro Is Created

Our process guarantees cadastral maps that meet the Registro Nacional's requirements:

1. Planning and Reconnaissance

We visit the property to identify ground control points, obstacles, safe flight zones, and special terrain features. This ensures an optimal flight plan and complete data collection.

2. Ground Control Points

We place precision ground control points (GCPs) using high-accuracy RTK GPS equipment. These georeferenced points anchor the drone data for maximum accuracy and legal validation.

3. Flight and Data Capture

The Matrice 300 RTK flies parallel flight lines at an optimized altitude (typically 50–100 meters), capturing high-resolution RGB imagery with stereo overlap for photogrammetry. Complementary LiDAR data provides precise elevation information that penetrates vegetation.

4. Processing and Map Generation

Data is processed in specialized software (Pix4D, Agisoft, or similar) to create a 3D point cloud, digital terrain model (DTM), georeferenced orthomosaic, and finally the technical plano catastro with certified dimensions, measurements, and areas.

5. Validation and Delivery

The final map is validated against Registro Nacional requirements, produced in compatible technical formats (DXF, PDF), and delivered with RTK accuracy certification. We include ground-control documentation and an uncertainty report.

DGAC Legal Requirements for Cadastral Drone Surveys

In Costa Rica, any commercial drone operation requires approval from the Dirección General de Aviación Civil (DGAC). For cadastral surveys, we comply with all current requirements:

DGAC Regulations 2026
  • Commercial operator license (LOC-001 or equivalent)
  • Aircraft certification (airworthiness)
  • Approval of specific flight areas
  • Liability insurance of at least US$100,000
  • Certified personnel documentation
  • An operations plan for every mission
  • Coordination with local authorities in restricted zones

Restricted Zones in Costa Rica

Some areas require extra permits or carry restrictions:

  • Airports: San José (SJO), Liberia (LIR), and Quepos (XQP) have 10 km exclusion zones
  • Public security zones: Military installations, prisons, and ports require special authorization
  • Protected areas: National parks and biological reserves require MINAE permits
  • Border zone: Within 2 km of the border requires Defense authorization

We handle all the DGAC permits your cadastral survey needs — you don't have to worry about regulatory compliance.

What a Plano Catastro Is Used For in Costa Rica

Property Registration

The most common case: you need to register a property with the Registro Nacional. A drone-based plano catastro meets the technical and accuracy requirements and speeds up the registration process.

Land Subdivision

If you plan to subdivide a property, the drone produces precise surveys of the full extent, letting you plan lots, access roads, and services accurately. That prevents legal conflicts later.

Construction and Development

For real-estate or construction projects, an aerial cadastral survey provides a precise topographic base for architectural design, volume calculations, and access planning — including elevation data that ground methods can't easily capture.

Boundary Disputes

When property limits are contested, an aerial cadastral survey provides independent documentation of the current boundaries, useful for legal proceedings or amicable demarcation.

Real-Estate Due Diligence

Buyers and appraisers use aerial cadastral maps with orthomosaics to evaluate properties, detect unregistered structures, and validate actual conditions against older documents.

Agricultural Projects

For farms and plantations, an aerial plano catastro includes precision topographic data useful for drainage design, irrigation, and internal access planning.
