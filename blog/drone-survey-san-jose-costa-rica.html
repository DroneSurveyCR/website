What People Use Drone Surveys For in San José

The GAM has a specific set of challenges that make drone surveys especially useful. The terrain is hilly and varied — a flat lot in Santa Ana sits very differently from a hillside condominium site in Escazú or a steep road cut in Desamparados. Traditional surveying methods struggle with these conditions. A drone equipped with lidar handles them in a fraction of the time.

Here's what we're typically hired to do in the San José area:

Construction Monitoring

Developers and project managers use our surveys to track earthwork progress, calculate cut and fill volumes, and make sure the site matches the approved plans. A single flight gives you a complete picture of where the project stands — far more useful than a tape measure and a lot more defensible if there's a dispute with your contractor.

Topographic Surveys for Building Permits

SETENA and municipal offices in San José require topographic surveys for most new construction projects. Our deliverables — contour maps, DXF files, GeoTIFFs — are formatted to work directly with your architect's or engineer's software. We include everything you need for your permit submission.

Subdivision and Cadastral Mapping

Dividing a property? Our aerial surveys give you accurate boundary data and elevation models that your land surveyor can use to finalize the subdivision plan. We work alongside licensed topographers regularly — we handle the aerial data collection, they handle the legal registration.

Road and Infrastructure Projects

Road design, drainage planning, retaining wall placement — all of these need accurate elevation data. The Ruta 27 corridor and the roads feeding into it have seen steady infrastructure investment, and we've mapped dozens of projects in that area.

Commercial Real Estate Assessment

Before buying land in Escazú or Santa Ana, a drone survey tells you exactly what you're dealing with — slope percentages, drainage patterns, buildable area. It's a small investment that can save you from a very expensive mistake.

The GAM Terrain Challenge: Why Lidar Often Makes More Sense Here

San José sits in a valley at about 1,100 meters elevation, surrounded by hills on all sides. The terrain drops sharply toward the south in places like Alajuelita, and rises steeply toward the east in Moravia and Coronado. A lot of the hillside land you'll encounter in the GAM has secondary vegetation — scrub, trees, or overgrown grass — that blocks photogrammetry from seeing the actual ground surface.

That's where lidar is worth the extra cost. The laser pulses pass through gaps in vegetation and return data from the bare earth below. For hillside construction sites or properties with any significant tree cover, lidar gives you the ground truth that photogrammetry simply can't.

For flat, open sites — parking lots, cleared construction pads, agricultural fields — photogrammetry works great and costs less. We'll tell you honestly which method your project needs when you request a quote.

Yes. We serve the entire GAM including San José city, Escazú, Santa Ana, Alajuelita, Desamparados, La Uruca, Moravia, Curridabat, Tres Ríos, and surrounding cantons. Projects in the GAM are included in our standard pricing with no travel surcharge.

How long does a drone survey take in San José?

Most projects in the San José area are completed in one to two field days, depending on size and site complexity. Data processing and delivery of your final files typically takes 3 to 5 business days after the flight. Rush delivery is available if you need it faster.
