What Aerial Photography Is Actually Used For in Costa Rica

Aerial imagery is not a luxury add-on here — it is often the single most effective marketing asset a property has. The most common uses we see:

  • Real estate listings. A single hero shot showing a lot’s position relative to the ocean, the road, and the neighboring land does more than a gallery of ground photos. For raw land especially, an aerial is the only way a remote buyer can grasp shape, slope, and setting.
  • Hotels, resorts & tourism. Beachfront properties, eco-lodges, and adventure operators use sweeping aerial video to convey scale and surroundings on their websites, OTAs, and social channels.
  • Construction & development progress. Developers document a project month by month from the same vantage points to show investors and buyers how the build is advancing.
  • Land & subdivision marketing. A georeferenced aerial with lot lines overlaid helps buyers visualize a parcel within a larger development.
  • Events & weddings. Destination weddings and corporate events at Costa Rica’s resorts increasingly include aerial coverage.

For real estate specifically, aerial imagery pairs naturally with our deeper drone services for real estate in Costa Rica — and when a buyer moves past the marketing stage, the same property often needs accurate boundary verification before closing.

Why it converts: Listings with aerial imagery consistently attract more views and faster engagement than ground-only galleries. For a remote, view-driven market like Costa Rica, the aerial often is the listing.

The Rules: Drone Photography and the DGAC

Costa Rica regulates drone flying, and commercial photography is squarely commercial use. The governing body is the DGAC (Dirección General de Aviación Civil). The essentials every client should know:

  • Registration & authorization. Drones above the weight threshold must be registered, and pilots flying commercially must be authorized. A professional operator already holds this — so when you hire one, you are not the one taking the legal risk.
  • Airspace restrictions. Flying near the international airports at San José (SJO) and Liberia (LIR), and near smaller aerodromes, is restricted. So is flying over national parks and protected wildlife areas without specific permission.
  • Altitude and line-of-sight. There are maximum altitude limits and a requirement to keep the aircraft within visual line of sight in most conditions.
  • Crowds and privacy. Flying over gatherings of people and capturing third parties without regard to privacy is restricted.
Don’t risk your own drone: Tourists and owners flying personal drones for “just a few listing photos” are technically flying commercially. Operating without authorization can lead to fines and confiscation. Hiring an authorized operator removes that exposure entirely.

Drone Photography vs. a Drone Survey: Which Do You Need?

This is the question that saves clients money. The two services overlap in the air but diverge completely in the output.

Drone Photography

Optimized for how a place looks. The pilot chooses angles, light, and movement to make the property compelling. Deliverables are edited photos and color-graded video — assets for listings, websites, and social media. Accuracy of position is irrelevant; emotional impact is everything.

Drone Survey

Optimized for what a place measures. The drone flies a precise grid with RTK GPS correction, producing georeferenced orthophotos, 3D point clouds, and topographic maps accurate to a few centimeters — data for engineering, boundaries, drainage, and earthwork volumes. Our topographic drone survey and LiDAR canopy mapping services cover this end.

The good news: A single mobilization can often capture both. We can shoot marketing-grade imagery and collect survey-grade data on the same visit — one trip, two deliverables. If you are weighing the technical options, our comparison of LiDAR vs. photogrammetry explains how the survey data is produced.

If you only need to make a property look its best for marketing, drone photography is all you need. If you need to plan, permit, build, or verify a boundary, you need a survey. Many developments need both at different stages.

How to Plan a Shoot That Actually Delivers

The difference between forgettable aerials and images that sell comes down to planning. A few things to get right before the drone leaves the ground:

  1. Shoot at golden hour. The hour after sunrise and before sunset gives warm light and long shadows that reveal terrain. Early morning in Costa Rica also brings the calmest air and clearest skies before afternoon clouds build — especially important in the green season (May–November).
  2. Know your hero shot. Decide the one image that has to land — usually the property in context with its view. Everything else supports it.
  3. Clear the site. Move vehicles, open gates, tidy the grounds. Aerials are unforgiving of clutter that ground photos hide.
  4. Check the airspace early. Proximity to an airport, a national park, or a protected area can change what is possible. A professional operator confirms this before booking, not on the day.
  5. Match the format to the platform. Vertical cuts for social, wide 16:9 for websites and listings, stills for print. Brief these up front so the edit serves every channel.

Frequently Asked Questions About Drone Photography in Costa Rica

How much does drone photography cost in Costa Rica?

A single-location real estate or property photo shoot typically starts around $300–$500 USD for a set of edited high-resolution aerials, and $500–$900 for a package that adds 4K video. Hotels, resorts, and multi-lot developments are quoted by scope and travel from San José. Use our online calculator for an instant estimate for your location.

Do I need a permit for drone photography in Costa Rica?

Commercial drone work is regulated by the DGAC. Drones over the weight threshold must be registered and commercial pilots must be authorized, with restrictions near airports, national parks, and crowds. A professional operator already holds the required authorization and insurance, so you don’t need your own permit when you hire one. Flying your own drone commercially without authorization risks fines and confiscation.

What’s the difference between drone photography and a drone survey?

Drone photography produces marketing-grade photos and video to show a property at its best. A drone survey produces measurable, georeferenced data — orthophotos, point clouds, and topographic maps accurate to a few centimeters — for engineering, boundaries, and volumes. Many projects need both, and the same flight can often capture imagery for each.

What is the best time of day for aerial photos in Costa Rica?

The golden hours just after sunrise and before sunset give the warmest light and most dramatic results. Early morning also brings the calmest air and clearest skies before afternoon clouds build, especially in the green season (May–November). For even, shadow-free midday shots, an overcast sky can also work well.

Can you shoot aerial photos anywhere in Costa Rica?

We fly nationwide — Guanacaste, the Central Valley, the South Pacific and Osa, the Caribbean, and beyond. The main limits are legal: airspace near the San José and Liberia airports, national parks and protected areas, and temporary restrictions over events. We check the airspace for your specific site before booking and plan around any restrictions.
