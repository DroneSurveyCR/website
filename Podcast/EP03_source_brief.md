## Key Talking Points

### 1. What is a topographic survey and why does it matter?
- Measures the shape of the land: elevation, slopes, contours, features
- Foundation of every construction, development, and engineering project
- Traditional method: survey crew walking the site for days with a total station
- Drone method: fly a grid pattern, capture millions of measurements in minutes
- Result: complete terrain data for the entire property, not just selected points

### 2. What deliverables do you actually get?
- **DTM (Digital Terrain Model)** — bare earth, vegetation removed. What engineers need for grading and drainage.
- **DSM (Digital Surface Model)** — everything on the surface including trees and buildings. Useful for canopy analysis, solar studies.
- **Contour lines** — at 0.25m, 0.5m, or 1m intervals. Required for most architectural and engineering plans in Costa Rica.
- **Orthophoto** — high-res aerial image with real-world coordinates at every pixel.
- **3D point cloud** — millions of points in LAS/LAZ format for CAD/GIS/BIM.
- **Slope analysis** — color-coded gradient map showing buildable areas and erosion risk.
- Optional: volumetrics, cut-and-fill, cross-sections, watershed models.

### 3. LiDAR vs photogrammetry for topographic work
- LiDAR: ±1–3 cm accuracy, penetrates vegetation canopy, produces bare-earth DTM under forest
- Photogrammetry: ±3–5 cm accuracy, lower cost, excellent for open terrain
- Costa Rica's mixed terrain (partly cleared, partly forested) often favors LiDAR
- Equipment: DJI Matrice 300 RTK with Zenmuse L1 (240,000 laser pulses/second)

### 4. How the survey process works (5 steps)
1. Pre-flight planning — check DGAC airspace, plan grid, choose sensor
2. Ground control points — GPS base station, surveyed reference markers
3. Drone flight — pre-programmed grid at 60–100m altitude, single morning for <50 ha
4. Data processing — point cloud classification, surface generation, quality control
5. Deliverable production — DWG, GeoTIFF, LAS, PDF formats, 72-hour standard turnaround

### 5. Real-world applications in Costa Rica
- Residential/commercial development: slope analysis identifies buildable zones (e.g., Escazu, Santa Ana, Atenas)
- Road and infrastructure: cut-and-fill calculations for road alignment
- Construction monitoring: monthly drone flights track earthwork progress
- Agriculture: irrigation design, terrace planning, erosion risk on coffee hillsides
- Environmental: SETENA requires topographic data for environmental impact assessments
- Real estate due diligence: verify buildability before buying undeveloped land

### 6. Drone topographic survey vs traditional ground survey
- Speed: 1 morning + 2 days vs 3–5 days fieldwork
- Data density: millions of points vs hundreds
- Accuracy: ±1–3 cm (LiDAR) vs ±1–2 cm (total station) — comparable
- Steep terrain: drone has no access issues
- Cost (10 ha): $1,400–$1,800 drone vs $2,000–$4,000+ traditional
- Legal note: plano catastro still needs a licensed topógrafo, but many now use drone data as their base layer

### 7. Pricing (2026)
- Base: $1,000 USD for up to 5 hectares
- Additional: $80/ha beyond 5 ha
- Travel fee: $50–$200 depending on distance from San José
- Terrain surcharge: 15–25% for steep slopes or dense vegetation
- All data delivered in CRTM05 coordinate system (EPSG:5367)
