## SOURCE MATERIAL

### The Challenge: Farming in Costa Rica's Diverse Terrain

Costa Rica is one of the world's most productive agricultural nations relative to its size. Pineapples, bananas, coffee, palm oil, sugarcane, macadamia — the country exports billions in crops to global markets every year. But here's the thing most people don't realize: farming in Costa Rica is incredibly complex because of the terrain.

You've got steep mountain slopes in the Central Valley where coffee grows. Humid lowlands on the Caribbean coast where banana plantations stretch for kilometers. River valleys, seasonal streams, ravines — conditions that can change dramatically within a single farm. And that makes traditional agricultural management really hard.

Think about it: if you're managing a 50-hectare pineapple field, there's no way a person walking the rows can see every plant. They might spot obvious problems — a section that looks yellow, a patch that's clearly wilting — but by the time crop stress is visible to the naked eye, you've already lost weeks of growing time and potentially a significant portion of your yield.

That's where drone surveys come in. A drone can map an entire 50-hectare field in hours, at centimeter-level resolution, and flag problems before they become losses.

### What Drone Surveys Actually Measure on a Farm

There are several key things a drone survey reveals that ground scouting simply can't match.

**Crop Health Mapping with NDVI**: This is probably the most powerful application. NDVI stands for Normalized Difference Vegetation Index — it's a way of measuring plant health using near-infrared and visible red light. Healthy plants absorb red light for photosynthesis and reflect near-infrared light. Stressed or dying plants do the opposite. The drone's multispectral camera captures both wavelengths and creates a color-coded health map of your entire crop — pixel by pixel.

The key insight is timing. NDVI maps can reveal crop stress, nutrient deficiencies, water problems, and even disease hotspots weeks before they're visible to the human eye. That early warning lets you apply targeted treatment to specific zones instead of blanket-spraying an entire field. Better for the crop, better for the environment, and much better for your bottom line.

**Irrigation and Drainage Analysis**: Accurate topographic surveys identify low spots where water pools, slopes that drain too quickly, and terrain features that affect irrigation coverage. When you combine this topographic data with NDVI data, you can directly correlate crop stress patterns with water distribution issues — pinpointing broken drip lines, blocked channels, and poorly designed drainage before you lose a full season.

**Farm Boundary and Area Verification**: This one is surprisingly important in Costa Rica. Many farms were registered with outdated area calculations. A GPS-accurate boundary survey confirms your actual planted area for export certifications, bank financing, crop insurance, and lease agreements. It's a defensible, current measurement that protects your business.

**Terrain Modeling for Infrastructure**: Planning new access roads? Expanding irrigation networks? Designing drainage systems? A drone topographic survey gives your engineers the 3D terrain data they need. Slopes, ravines, and seasonal streams that don't appear on any existing map become clearly visible in a drone-derived Digital Elevation Model.

**Yield Estimation**: High-resolution aerial photos allow canopy coverage analysis, plant density counts, and biomass estimation. For coffee and pineapple operations, repeat surveys at key growth stages can inform harvest planning, logistics staffing, and export volume projections.

**Forest Inventory and FONAFIFO Compliance**: Many Costa Rica farms have forest reserves, natural corridors, or FONAFIFO payment agreements that require accurate area calculations and vegetation cover documentation. LiDAR drone surveys penetrate the forest canopy to map the ground beneath — providing defensible measurements for carbon credit programs, conservation easements, and regulatory compliance.

### Who Should Be Using This Technology?

Large pineapple operations in the South Pacific and Osa regions. Coffee cooperatives in the Central Valley and Tarrazú. Banana plantations on the Caribbean coast. Palm oil operations in Quepos and Parrita. Sugarcane fields in Guanacaste. Even small farms and cooperatives — group pricing means multiple properties surveyed in a single campaign reduces the per-hectare cost significantly.

### How the Survey Process Works

It's straightforward. First, we plan the mission — reviewing the property via satellite, confirming airspace requirements, and designing the flight grid. Then we place GPS ground control points across the farm for accurate georeferencing. The drone flies with RGB and multispectral cameras, capturing overlapping imagery at consistent altitude. Most farms up to 50 hectares are completed in a single day.

Processing takes 2 to 4 business days. Our GIS team produces orthophotos, digital elevation models, NDVI maps, and any additional analysis. Everything is delivered via Google Drive with a written interpretation of the NDVI findings and flags for areas requiring immediate attention.
