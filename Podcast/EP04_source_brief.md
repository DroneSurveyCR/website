## Key Talking Points

### 1. What is a SETENA EIA and who actually needs one?

- SETENA is Costa Rica's national environmental permitting authority — no significant development goes forward without it
- EIAs aren't just for big industrial projects: hotels, housing developments over 50 units, commercial centers, even some smaller projects trigger the requirement
- The four categories (A, B1, B2, C) determine how much documentation is required
- Category A (factories, large resorts, highways) requires the full works — specialist studies, detailed spatial data, drainage models
- Category B1 (hotels, commercial centers, housing developments) needs an abbreviated EIA but still requires comprehensive mapping data
- Most developers don't realize how data-heavy the EIA filing actually is until they're already under time pressure from a financing deadline

### 2. The six datasets SETENA actually needs from a drone survey

1. **Georeferenced orthophoto** — a high-res aerial image mosaic of the whole property in Costa Rica's CRTM05 coordinate system. Think of it as Google Earth for your specific property, but taken last week and accurate to 2–5 cm per pixel instead of images that might be 2 years old
2. **Digital Terrain Model (DTM) + contour maps** — bare-earth elevation stripped of vegetation, contour lines at 0.5–1m intervals. This is what engineers use to design drainage and what SETENA uses to assess slope and erosion risk
3. **Slope and hazard analysis maps** — color-coded maps showing flat, gentle, moderate, steep, and very steep zones. Slopes over 15% and over 30% trigger specific SETENA review criteria
4. **Drainage basin and watershed delineation** — where does rainwater flow? Where does it concentrate? What natural watercourses does it feed? This is technically the most demanding piece of the EIA and the one drone surveys handle far better than ground crews
5. **Vegetation coverage and land use map** — what's there now? Primary forest, secondary growth, pasture, wetlands, developed area. Critical for properties near national parks, biological corridors, or wetlands
6. **Property boundary survey** — georeferenced confirmation of the exact parcel boundaries, matched to the cadastral plan

### 3. Why LiDAR changes everything for vegetated properties

- Standard photogrammetry drones map the top of the tree canopy, not the ground underneath
- In Costa Rica's humid tropical terrain — which is often partly forested, partly cleared — you absolutely need to know what's under those trees
- LiDAR fires 240,000 laser pulses per second and captures multiple returns per pulse, so it literally sees through canopy gaps to the actual ground
- For EIAs involving forested buffer zones, watercourse setbacks, or jungle-adjacent land, LiDAR is the only way to get accurate terrain data without actually cutting vegetation — which you'd then need SETENA permission to do anyway
- This is why we use the DJI Zenmuse L1: it's one of the most capable commercial LiDAR systems operating in Costa Rica

### 4. The actual process — day by day

- **Day 0:** Client provides property boundaries (cadastral plan). We register the flight plan with DGAC (Costa Rica's civil aviation authority) and confirm airspace clearance. For sites near airports or restricted zones, we get advance authorization — typically 24–48 hours
- **Day 1 (field mission):** Our pilot and ground crew mobilize to the property. We set up RTK GPS base station control points for centimeter-accurate georeferencing. The drone flies automated grid patterns over the property plus a buffer zone (typically 50–100m beyond the boundary, as SETENA requires). LiDAR collects point cloud and imagery simultaneously in a single pass
- **Days 2–3 (processing + QC):** Raw data becomes orthophoto, DTM, contour maps, slope maps, drainage delineation. A senior technician reviews accuracy, datum, projection, and completeness
- **Day 3–4 (deliverable package):** Everything lands in your inbox in SETENA-ready formats — GeoTIFF for rasters, DXF for vectors, LAS/LAZ point clouds, KMZ for Google Earth review, PDF maps for the printed report, and a full accuracy certification document

### 5. Post-approval compliance — the use case people forget

- SETENA approval is not the end of the relationship — most Category A and B1 approvals come with ongoing monitoring obligations
- Quarterly or annual drone surveys create a timestamped, georeferenced record showing: clearing extent vs. approved limits, buffer zone maintenance, erosion control effectiveness, revegetation progress, and stormwater drainage condition
- This documented aerial record is your protection in a SETENA inspection or third-party complaint — it eliminates the ambiguity that leads to enforcement actions
- Many developers who use us for the initial EIA survey become recurring compliance clients

### 6. Pricing — transparent and practical

- Up to 5 ha: $1,000 USD flat — includes the full deliverable package (orthophoto, DTM, contours, slope, drainage, accuracy report)
- Above 5 ha: $80/ha for each additional hectare
- 100+ ha: custom project rates
- Travel fee applies based on distance from San José
- Expedited 24-hour processing available for urgent EIA deadlines (additional fee)
- For projects needing formal hydrological reports signed by licensed engineers, we coordinate with our network of environmental consultants in Costa Rica

**Topic:** How drone surveys accelerate Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) filings in Costa Rica under the SETENA permitting system.

**The big problem this solves:** Developers building in Costa Rica need Environmental Impact Assessment approval from SETENA before they can break ground on most significant projects. The spatial data SETENA requires — topographic maps, drainage basin models, vegetation coverage analysis, slope hazard maps — used to take weeks to compile with traditional ground survey teams. Drone surveys compress that timeline from weeks to 72 hours, making EIA data collection a fast-path item rather than a critical-path bottleneck.

**Who SETENA is:** SETENA — the Secretaría Técnica Nacional Ambiental — administers Costa Rica's environmental permitting system. The agency classifies development projects into four categories based on environmental risk. Category A (industrial plants, large resorts, highways) requires a full EIA with specialist studies. Category B1 (hotels, commercial centers, housing developments over 50 units) requires an abbreviated EIA with technical studies. Category B2 needs an environmental management plan. Category C only requires a sworn statement. For Category A and B1 projects especially, detailed spatial data is non-negotiable.

**The six datasets drone surveys provide for SETENA:**

One: A georeferenced orthophoto. This is a high-resolution aerial image mosaic of the property, stitched from hundreds of drone photographs and georeferenced to CRTM05 — Costa Rica's national coordinate system. Resolution is typically 2 to 5 centimeters per pixel, far superior to Google Earth imagery that may be years old. SETENA uses the orthophoto to assess current land use, vegetation condition, water bodies, and proximity to adjacent protected areas.

Two: A Digital Terrain Model and contour maps. The DTM shows bare-earth elevation stripped of all vegetation cover. From it, contour lines at 0.5 or 1 meter intervals are generated — standard requirement for SETENA drainage and slope assessment. The key word is "bare-earth": this is where LiDAR becomes critical on forested properties.

Three: Slope classification and hazard maps. Derived from the DTM, these color-coded maps classify terrain into flat, gentle, moderate, steep, and very steep gradient categories. Properties with slopes exceeding 15 percent or 30 percent trigger specific SETENA review criteria and may require detailed stabilization planning documentation.

Four: Drainage basin and watershed delineation. One of the most technically demanding EIA requirements. SETENA needs to understand exactly how rainfall flows across and off the property — where it concentrates, what natural watercourses it feeds, and how development will alter drainage patterns. Drone-derived terrain models allow precise delineation of sub-watershed boundaries, natural flow paths, and runoff volume calculations that feed the stormwater management design.

Five: Vegetation coverage and land use map. SETENA requires documentation of existing vegetation types and coverage percentages — primary forest, secondary growth, pasture, crops, wetlands, and developed areas. This is particularly critical for properties near national parks, biological corridors, or wetlands, where clearing limits and buffer zone regulations are strictly enforced.

Six: A georeferenced property boundary survey. Precise, legally verifiable confirmation of the subject parcel boundaries, matched to the registered cadastral plan and produced using RTK GPS. This defines the spatial extent of the entire environmental study.

**Why LiDAR matters on Costa Rica's vegetated terrain:** Standard photogrammetry drones map the surface they can see — which on a forested property is the tree canopy, not the ground. Costa Rica's mixed terrain (partly cleared, partly forested) means photogrammetry alone will produce a terrain model that looks like a continuous green carpet rather than the actual hillside beneath. A LiDAR system fires 240,000 laser pulses per second and captures multiple returns per pulse, penetrating canopy gaps to reach actual ground level. This is the only way to produce an accurate bare-earth DTM on a vegetated property without physically clearing vegetation — which would require separate SETENA authorization to do legally.

The team registers the flight mission with DGAC (Costa Rica's civil aviation authority) and obtains airspace clearance — 24 to 48 hours for sites near airports or restricted corridors. Day one: the pilot and ground crew mobilize to the property. An RTK GPS base station establishes centimeter-accurate ground control. The drone flies automated grid patterns over the full property plus a 50 to 100 meter buffer zone beyond the boundary. LiDAR simultaneously collects point cloud data and imagery in a single pass. Properties up to 200 hectares can be covered in one flight day. Days two and three: raw flight data is processed into orthophoto, DTM, slope maps, contour maps, and drainage delineation. A senior technician performs quality control checking accuracy, coordinate system, datum, and completeness before packaging. Days three and four: deliverables are packaged in SETENA-ready formats — GeoTIFF rasters, DXF vectors, LAS and LAZ point clouds, KMZ files for Google Earth preview, PDF maps for the printed report, and a formal accuracy certification document. Everything is referenced to CRTM05 with complete coordinate metadata.

**Post-approval compliance monitoring:** Most Category A and B1 SETENA approvals include ongoing monitoring obligations. Regular drone surveys — typically quarterly or annual — produce timestamped, georeferenced aerial records documenting clearing extents versus approved limits, buffer zone maintenance, erosion control effectiveness, revegetation progress, and drainage system condition.
