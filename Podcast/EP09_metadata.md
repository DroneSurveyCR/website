## Spotify Description

What does a drone topographic survey actually give you in Costa Rica — and why does it save so much money? In this episode we break down terrain mapping from the air: DTMs and DSMs, contour lines, slope and drainage analysis, and the cut-and-fill calculations that decide whether a project pencils out.

**Topics covered:**
- What a topographic survey is, and how a drone captures millions of points vs a ground crew's thousands
- DTM vs DSM explained — bare earth vs top-of-everything, and why the DTM is the prize for engineering
- Accuracy: ±1–3 cm with LiDAR, ±3–5 cm photogrammetry vs ±1–2 cm total station — same ballpark, a fraction of the time
- The full deliverable set: contour lines, orthophoto, slope/aspect maps, drainage analysis, point cloud
- Cut-and-fill: balancing earthwork before machines arrive, and why it's the biggest cost lever
- Costa Rica's 30% slope development restriction — knowing your buildable zones first
- Who needs it: construction, land subdivision, architects, CFIA and SETENA permitting
- "Do I still need a topógrafo?" — the legal boundary vs the terrain data, and how they work together
- Regional spotlight: Central Valley hillsides, Osa/South Pacific forest, large Guanacaste sites
- Cost: from $1,000 per 5 hectares, and why surveying first beats fixing later

If you're buying, developing, or building on land in Costa Rica, this episode explains the survey data your engineer and architect actually need.
