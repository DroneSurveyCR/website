## Spotify Description

A clean title tells you the seller owns the land. It does not tell you what that land actually is. In this episode we break down how a single drone flight verifies your Costa Rica property boundaries before you close — comparing the physical land against the registered plano catastrado and catching the overlaps, encroachments, and missing markers that traditional due diligence misses.

**Topics covered:**
- Costa Rica's two property systems: Registro Nacional (title) vs Catastro Nacional (the cadastral map and your real area, the cabida)
- Why the map so often doesn't match the ground: old survey methods, lost or moved mojones, overlapping plans (traslapes), fences built by eye, informal subdivisions
- The reveal: none of this shows up in a title search
- How a drone verifies boundaries — RTK aerial mapping georeferenced to CRTM05, locating mojones, overlaying the registered plano, and the discrepancy report (1–5 cm accuracy)
- The legal line: a drone survey supports — but does not replace — a licensed topógrafo's CFIA-stamped plano
- What it catches before you buy: area shortfall, neighbor encroachment, traslape, missing legal access (servidumbre), Maritime Zone overlap, river setbacks, steep-slope limits
- Where it matters most for foreign buyers: South Pacific/Osa, Guanacaste coast, Central Valley foothills, Caribbean/Limón
- How to fit a boundary survey into your purchase, during due diligence and before closing
- Cost: from $1,000 for up to 5 hectares — a rounding error against the price of the land

If you're buying land in Costa Rica, this episode could save you tens of thousands of dollars and years of legal disputes.
