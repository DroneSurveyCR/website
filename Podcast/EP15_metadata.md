## Spotify Description

Costa Rica's Caribbean coast is a different world — flat, low-lying land near sea level, buried under dense year-round rainforest, where a 40-centimeter rise decides whether you flood. In this episode we explain why lidar is the only survey technology that works here, and how the same flight serves eco-lodges in Puerto Viejo, the port at Limón, and the banana farms of the lowlands.

**Topics covered:**
- Why the Caribbean side is a different world: rainforest, estuaries, mangroves, and fast-accelerating tourism and port development
- The double problem — flat low-lying land plus dense vegetation that hides the true ground surface
- Why lidar beats photogrammetry here: laser pulses penetrate the canopy where cameras only see the treetops (DJI Zenmuse L1, centimeter-accuracy DEM)
- A real Playa Cocles eco-lodge case: a 40 cm building ridge found in one morning that ground crews couldn't reach in three weeks
- SETENA and protected-area buffers near Cahuita, Gandoca-Manzanillo, and Tortuguero — knowing your buildable footprint first
- Port logistics at Limón and Moín: stockpile volumes, progress monitoring, drainage design, environmental mapping
- Caribbean agriculture: banana drainage canals, plantation replanting, and Talamanca cacao mapping for Bribri and Cabécar farmers
- Caribbean weather for drone flights: 3,000–4,000 mm/yr, morning flyable windows, and the September–October dry stretch
- Coverage from Tortuguero to Sixaola, and pricing from $1,000 per 5 hectares

If you're buying, building, or managing property anywhere on Costa Rica's Caribbean coast, this episode explains the land data your eyes simply can't give you.
