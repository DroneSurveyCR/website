## SOURCE MATERIAL (Conversational Podcast-Ready Tone)

So, if you're thinking about getting a drone survey done in Costa Rica, the first question that comes to mind is usually: *How much is this going to cost me?* And honestly, that's one of the trickiest questions in the industry because the price can vary wildly depending on what you actually need.

Let me break down what we're seeing in the market right now. If you're looking at photogrammetry—that's basically using high-resolution photos to create 3D models—you're typically looking at somewhere between $1,500 and $5,000 for a standard project. It's a great option if you need good detail but don't have a huge budget.

LiDAR, on the other hand, is the heavier hitter. That's laser-based mapping, and it's incredibly accurate for dense terrain or complex topography. You're going to spend between $3,000 and $8,000 on that kind of survey. It costs more, but you're getting precision that's hard to match with other methods.

Then there's topographic surveying. This is especially useful if you're doing construction or real estate due diligence. That'll run you $2,000 to $6,000 depending on the area and complexity.

And if you just need a quick inspection—maybe you're checking out a structure, a landslide, or something like that—you can get away with $800 to $3,000. That's the entry point for a lot of projects.

Now, here's where it gets interesting. The cost isn't just about the method you choose. It's about the terrain you're surveying. If you're in Guanacaste working with relatively flat land, that's going to be cheaper and faster than trying to map the dense jungle and steep slopes of the Osa Peninsula. Weather also matters a lot. If you're surveying during rainy season, that complicates things. Accessibility—how easy it is to get equipment to the site—that affects cost. And the level of accuracy you need, plus processing time, all play a role.

Let me give you some real-world examples. We've done projects in Guanacaste where a real estate developer needed drone surveying for a new residential development. That was straightforward terrain, standard photogrammetry, came in around $2,500. But when we were mapping conservation land in the Osa Peninsula, the dense jungle and steep elevation changes meant we had to use LiDAR and extra processing time. That project was closer to $7,000.

One thing a lot of people ask is whether they can get a discount if they bundle multiple services or do repeat surveys. The answer is yes, there's definitely flexibility there. If you're doing quarterly monitoring of a construction site, you'll negotiate a better per-survey rate than if you're doing a one-off job.

Another common question: Can you really compare drone surveys to traditional surveying? The short answer is yes, but they do different things well. Drones are faster and cheaper for large areas, but traditional surveying might be better for precise property boundary work. Most serious projects use both.
