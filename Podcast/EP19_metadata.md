## Spotify Description

A flat drone photo can't tell a buyer in Toronto or Munich what it actually feels like to stand on a jungle, ocean-view lot in Costa Rica. A new technique called Gaussian splatting can. In this episode we break down what 3D Gaussian splatting really is — in plain English — how it turns ordinary drone imagery into a photorealistic scene you can fly through in your browser, and exactly where it fits alongside the LiDAR and photogrammetry surveys we already do.

**Topics covered:**
- What Gaussian splatting (3DGS) actually is: drone photos → a photorealistic 3D scene rendered from millions of soft colored "splats," explorable in real time on a laptop or phone
- How fast it went mainstream: research in 2023 → built into mainstream survey software (SimActive Correlator3D) by May 2026
- The key distinction: LiDAR & photogrammetry are for MEASURING; Gaussian splatting is for SEEING — and the same flight can feed both
- Where it matters in Costa Rica: real estate sold sight-unseen to overseas buyers, tourism & eco-lodge previews, construction stakeholder review, heritage & conservation capture
- The honest limits: a splat is a visualization, not a measurement — not georeferenced, not centimeter-certified, and it cannot produce or replace a registered plano catastrado (that still needs a CFIA-stamped topógrafo + RTK/LiDAR/photogrammetry)
- How a splat is made: an overlapping, oblique drone flight → structure-from-motion → "trained" with tools like Postshot, Luma, gsplat or Correlator3D into a single browser-ready scene
- One flight, two deliverables: survey-grade maps AND a walkable 3D scene
- Where DroneSurveyCR stands: actively testing it, while core deliverables remain survey-grade LiDAR, photogrammetry, GPS and topographic mapping — centimeter-accurate since 2017

If you're selling land to buyers who can't visit, marketing a lodge, or just want to understand the most exciting shift in drone visualization in years, this episode is for you.
