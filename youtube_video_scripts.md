LIDAR VS PHOTOGRAMMETRY

### Video Script (5 min 10 sec - ~700 words)

Hi, I'm [Name] from Drone Survey CR. Today we're answering a question that comes up constantly: What's the difference between LiDAR and photogrammetry, and which one do you need?

If you're considering a drone survey for real estate, construction, agriculture, or government work, understanding these two technologies is critical to getting exactly the data you need. Let's break it down.

**What is LiDAR?**

LiDAR stands for Light Detection and Ranging. Think of it as a sophisticated laser system that fires thousands of laser pulses per second, measures how long each pulse takes to bounce back from the ground, and creates an incredibly detailed 3D map based on those measurements.

Our DJI Zenmuse L1 system measures distances with sub-centimeter accuracy—that's accurate within just a few millimeters. It works day or night, and it penetrates through vegetation, which is crucial in Costa Rica where jungle coverage is dense.

LiDAR gives you a point cloud—essentially millions of individual coordinate points that represent every surface it scanned. From that point cloud, you can calculate precise elevations, volumes, drainage patterns, and structural details with remarkable accuracy.

**What is Photogrammetry?**

Photogrammetry is different. Instead of lasers, it uses high-resolution photography. The drone takes hundreds of overlapping images from multiple angles and altitudes. Specialized software analyzes these images, identifies matching points between photos, and reconstructs a 3D model.

Photogrammetry is excellent at capturing visual detail and color. You get gorgeous orthomosaicked images—basically perfectly stitched aerial photos—that show exactly what things look like from above. It's fantastic for visual reference and for clients who want to see their property in high-quality color imagery.

**Accuracy: How Do They Compare?**

This is where the technologies diverge significantly.

LiDAR provides sub-centimeter accuracy—consistently accurate within 2 to 3 centimeters across the entire survey. This precision is essential for construction planning, grading operations, volumetric calculations for mining or earthwork, and any project where measurement accuracy directly impacts costs.

Photogrammetry typically achieves accuracy in the 5 to 10-centimeter range, depending on image resolution, overlap, and ground control points. It's still precise enough for most real estate and planning applications, but it's not as accurate as LiDAR for complex volumetric work.

**Cost Differences**

LiDAR typically costs more because the technology is more sophisticated and processing is more intensive. Our standard pricing—1,000 dollars for 5 hectares, plus 80 dollars per additional hectare—reflects LiDAR with professional processing.

Photogrammetry-only surveys would cost less, but here's the thing: we believe LiDAR delivers better value in Costa Rica's environment, so we typically recommend it. However, we can discuss photogrammetry-focused approaches if your budget is constrained and your accuracy needs are lower.

**When to Choose LiDAR**

Use LiDAR when you have dense vegetation—which is most of Costa Rica. If your property is covered in jungle, trees, or thick brush, LiDAR penetrates through that and captures the ground underneath. Photogrammetry struggles in dense vegetation because the camera can't see the ground.

Choose LiDAR for precision-critical projects: construction grading, volumetric calculations, precise elevation data, or anything where measurement accuracy directly impacts your budget or timeline.

LiDAR is also ideal when you need data in various weather conditions or when you can't conduct multiple flight passes due to airspace restrictions or time constraints.

**When to Choose Photogrammetry**

Photogrammetry shines when you need visual reference—you want people to see what your property actually looks like from above. Real estate marketing, site visualization, and client presentations benefit from high-quality color orthomosaics.

If vegetation is light to moderate and you need 3D visualization but not sub-centimeter accuracy, photogrammetry delivers excellent results at a lower cost.

For projects where stunning color imagery matters as much as numerical precision, photogrammetry is often the better choice.

**Our Recommendation**

Honestly, for most Costa Rica projects, we recommend LiDAR because vegetation is so prevalent. You get superior accuracy, better ground penetration, faster processing despite the dense jungle, and data that serves multiple future purposes.

But we work with your needs. If your project prioritizes visual imagery, if budget is the primary constraint, or if terrain is light with minimal vegetation, we'll design a photogrammetry solution that works.
